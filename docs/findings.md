# Findings

Preliminary results from the demand model (Phase 3). They will be expanded with SQL analysis and dashboards in later phases. All figures are modeled estimates; see [assumptions.md](assumptions.md).

## Market size

- The nine Northeast states spend an estimated **$16.8 billion** on residential heating oil and propane in the 2025-26 season: about **3.2 billion gallons** of heating oil and **1.0 billion gallons** of propane.
- New York is the largest state market ($4.7 billion), followed by Pennsylvania ($2.9 billion) and Massachusetts ($2.8 billion).
- The largest county markets are Suffolk County, NY ($686 million), Worcester County, MA ($574 million), Middlesex County, MA ($499 million), and Connecticut's Capitol Planning Region ($472 million).

## Seasonality

- Demand peaks in **January** in every county. December to February account for about half of the year's gallons, and the peak month runs 2.2 to 2.6 times the monthly average.
- Weather moves the market. Spend ranged from $14.3 billion (2024-25) to $16.8 billion (2025-26) across the five seasons; 2023-24, a very warm winter, had the lowest gallons.

## Year-round opportunity

- Scored on totals, the best year-round markets are the big counties around New York City and Boston, because both heating and cooling demand grow with the number of homes.
- Scored per home, the leaders are oil-heavy counties outside the big cities with warm summers: Sussex County, NJ; Northeastern Connecticut; Putnam County, NY; and the Lower Connecticut River Valley. This view points to where full-service dealers are most concentrated relative to population, which matters for the Connecticut dealer pilot.

## Model checks

- The cooling index tracks actual summer residential electricity sales (EIA 861M), with month-to-month correlations of 0.83 to 0.99 in every state except Maine (0.56), where few homes have air conditioning.
- Before calibration, the model is 8% to 40% below EIA's reported state totals for heating oil, which is expected because those totals include homes that use oil only as a backup or for hot water.
